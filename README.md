# USMLE Prep Pro Tools

### Personal study analytics for high-volume USMLE Q-bank preparation

USMLE Prep Pro Tools is a personal study-analytics toolkit I developed during my own USMLE Q-bank preparation to make question progress, focused study sessions, break patterns, session history, and circadian study patterns clearly visible.

What began as a reliable question counter evolved through sustained daily use into a broader system designed to structure concentration, optimize study timing, and support long-term performance.

## Why I Built This

The project started with a simple need: I wanted a reliable daily graph of how many Q-bank questions I was actually completing.

Without that visual record, progress felt vague. Some days felt productive, others did not, and it was difficult to see the difference clearly over time. Once I could see question volume graphically, the feedback itself became motivating and helped me understand which study days were working and which were not.

Around the same time, I was already using time-management software to track my activity and distraction-blocking tools to lock down my interface during study blocks. I wanted a system that held me accountable, not just one that logged data passively. The question tracker became one more part of that larger effort to protect and structure my focus.

As I used it, the limitations of a simple question counter became obvious. Different Q-bank and assessment workflows behaved differently, question counts could be duplicated or missed, and solving questions was not the same activity as reviewing them. That led to separate tracking for active question-solving and review, followed by repeated testing and refinement.

The project then grew around the larger problem I was actually trying to solve: sustaining concentration during long periods of USMLE preparation. I wanted to see not only how much work I completed, but how long I stayed focused, when I took breaks, how my study sessions changed across the day, and what patterns appeared over weeks of preparation.

What began as a question counter gradually became the foundation of USMLE Prep Pro Tools — a personal study-analytics system built around my own Q-bank workflow and refined through sustained daily use.

## How It Evolved

The toolkit grew gradually from real problems I encountered while studying, not from a fixed product plan — each stage added only after daily use exposed a limitation in the one before it.

**1. Question progress**  
It began with reliable question counting and a visual record of daily Q-bank volume, so progress was measurable rather than something I had to estimate from memory.

**2. Solving and review as separate workflows**  
I soon realized that answering questions and reviewing them were fundamentally different study activities, each with its own goals. That led to dedicated tracking for active question-solving and review, rather than a single combined count.

**3. Focus and break patterns**  
Question totals alone didn't explain why some sessions worked better than others. The system expanded to make focused work periods and break patterns visible alongside question progress, surfacing where concentration held up and where it broke down.

**4. Session history**  
Individual days were useful, but repeated sessions revealed far more. A historical view let me compare study behavior across days and recognize patterns that weren't visible day to day.

**5. Circadian study patterns**  
A later layer connected all of this to time of day — turning session history into a clear picture of when I tended to start studying, when longer uninterrupted study periods occurred, and how those patterns shifted over weeks of preparation.

Each stage came from a real gap exposed through everyday use, followed by repeated testing, correction, and refinement.
