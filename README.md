# USMLE Prep Pro Tools

### Personal study analytics for high-volume USMLE Q-bank preparation

USMLE Prep Pro Tools is a personal software toolkit I developed during my own USMLE Q-bank preparation.

The project began with a simple problem: completing large volumes of questions was not enough. I wanted to understand how I was actually studying — how long I could sustain focused work, how frequently I took breaks, when I studied best, and how those patterns changed over time.

What began as accurate question tracking gradually evolved into a broader study-analytics system combining question progress, focused study sessions, break patterns, session history, and circadian study-pattern visualization.

The goal was to turn everyday Q-bank activity into clear visual feedback designed to build concentration, optimize study timing, and support long-term performance.

## Why I Built This

The project started with a simple need: I wanted a reliable daily graph of how many Q-bank questions I was actually completing.

Without that visual record, progress felt vague. Some days felt productive, others did not, and it was difficult to see the difference clearly over time. Once I could see question volume graphically, the feedback itself became motivating and helped me understand which study days were working and which were not.

Around the same time, I was already using time-management software to track my activity and distraction-blocking tools to lock down my interface during study blocks. I wanted a system that held me accountable, not just one that logged data passively. The question tracker became one more part of that larger effort to protect and structure my focus.

As I used it, the limitations of a simple question counter became obvious. Different Q-bank and assessment workflows behaved differently, question counts could be duplicated or missed, and solving questions was not the same activity as reviewing them. That led to separate tracking for active question-solving and review, followed by repeated testing and refinement.

The project then grew around the larger problem I was actually trying to solve: sustaining concentration during long periods of USMLE preparation. I wanted to see not only how much work I completed, but how long I stayed focused, when I took breaks, how my study sessions changed across the day, and what patterns appeared over weeks of preparation.

What began as a question counter gradually became the foundation of USMLE Prep Pro Tools — a personal study-analytics system built around my own Q-bank workflow and refined through sustained daily use.
